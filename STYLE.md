# STYLE — Saagar's writing voice

Built from analysis of ~165 of his real sent emails (Aug–Sept 2026, filtered to exclude auto-generated content and drafts written by Jarvis itself), plus full-text reads of representative samples across registers: financial planning, vendor outreach, contract negotiation, institutional/credentialing correspondence, and quick internal replies.

Use this whenever drafting anything in his voice — replies, outreach, forms of address. The goal is a draft he'd send with light or no editing, not a stylistic pastiche.

## Greeting — depends on relationship and thread state

- **"Hi [Name],"** — the default for an ongoing professional relationship (advisors, coordinators, vendors he's spoken with before). Most common greeting by far.
- **"Hey [Name],"** — reserved for people he's more familiar with or has an easier rapport with (a colleague, a broker he's negotiated with directly).
- **"[Name],"** with no salutation word — mid-thread quick replies, especially once a few messages have gone back and forth.
- **"Dear [Name/Team],"** or **"To Whom It May Concern,"** — first contact with an institution, or formal credentialing/insurance/HR correspondence.
- Never "Hi there," "Hi team!," or anything with an exclamation point in the greeting.

## Opening line — almost always acknowledges first

Overwhelmingly starts a reply with gratitude or acknowledgment before content — "Thank you for..." and "Thanks for..." together account for the large majority of opening lines. Common patterns:
- "Thanks for the quick response / detailed breakdown / prompt response / quick turnaround."
- "Thank you for the info / for double checking and confirming / for reaching out."
- When *initiating* (not replying): "I hope you're doing well," "Hope this message finds you well," "Hope you've been well."

Only skips this when the email is purely transactional (forwarding a signed document, a one-line data point).

## Body structure

- **Numbers everything with more than one part.** Multiple questions or asks become a numbered list (1. 2. 3.), not run into prose. This is his single strongest structural habit.
- **Sub-points get bullet lists**, sometimes with a bolded or italicized label heading a cluster of related bullets (seen in contract-negotiation emails).
- **Precise over vague.** Exact dollar figures, exact dates, exact account/case numbers — never "around $X" when he has the real number. Heavy use of parentheticals to attach a clarifying aside without breaking the sentence: "(all W-2)", "(NYC office)", "(Limited trim)".
- **Sentence length tracks complexity, not a fixed style.** Short and direct for a simple ask or confirmation. Long, multi-clause sentences — often with an em dash or parenthetical mid-sentence — when explaining financial, contractual, or medical/administrative detail. Don't force artificial brevity onto genuinely complex content.
- Uses "quick" a lot as a softener/sizing word: "quick flag," "two quick things," "one other quick question."
- Softens asks with "I'd like to request," "hoping [X] can be accommodated," "kindly asking," particularly when negotiating something (contract terms, fee waivers).
- Closes the body — before signature — with a helpful offer when appropriate: "Happy to upload X — let me know what you need." / "Let me know if you need anything else."

## Sign-off

- **Standard default signature** (auto-appended to most emails, especially shorter/transactional ones):
  ```
  --
  Thank you for your time and consideration.

  Sincerely,
  Saagar Sheth
  ```
- **For longer, more personal or ongoing-relationship emails, this gets replaced with a short custom close** instead of the full block: "Thanks,\nSaagar" or similar. The custom close is more common in emails to financial/legal advisors he works with regularly.
- **For first-contact vendor outreach**, closes with "Best regards," followed by a full contact block:
  ```
  Best regards,
  Name: Saagar Sheth
  Number: 203-751-2755
  Email: saagarsheth@gmail.com
  ```
- Never signs off "Cheers," "Warmly," "Talk soon," or with emoji.

## Tone

Direct, businesslike, and polite without being effusive. No exclamation points except in genuinely casual personal messages (e.g., a "movie night?" text to his wife). Confident and specific rather than hedging — he states numbers, dates, and positions plainly, then asks a clear question. Warmth comes through as gratitude and acknowledgment (see opening line habit), not through enthusiasm or filler ("great!," "awesome," "so excited").

## Recurring phrases, ranked by real frequency in his sent mail

1. "Thank you for..." / "Thanks for..." (dominant opener)
2. "I hope this [email/message] finds you well" / "Hope you've been well" (initiating-contact opener)
3. "I wanted to..." (framing device for the ask)
4. "To confirm:" / "To clarify," (precision checkpoints)
5. "Reaching out" (self-describing the purpose of a first-contact email)
6. "Looking forward to..."
7. "Quick [flag/question/thing]" as a sizing word
